# 🎃 Quantum Trick or Treat

**The rules, in full. You do not need to read this to play.**

If ten people have ten minutes, hand them the game and let them press Play.
This document is for whoever is running the night, or for the one person at the
table who wants to know how the quantum part actually works.

---

## In one breath

Texas Hold'em. You bet candy. One of your two cards is face up for everyone to
see. Occasionally a card becomes a **?** that is genuinely two cards at once
until somebody looks at it. That is the whole game.

---

## Setting up

From the main menu: **🎃 Trick or Treat**, or press `H`.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **How long** | Quick Fright (8 hands, ~5 min) · Full Haunt (14, ~10 min) · All Hallows (22, ~15 min) |
| **How many monsters** | 1 to 5 opponents |
| **How weird** | Calm / Spooky / Cursed — how often the dealer interferes |
| **Who is playing** | One name for solo. Several names, comma separated, for party mode |

Everyone starts with **50 candy**. Nobody is ever eliminated.

## The hand

Ordinary Texas Hold'em, with one addition.

1. **Deal.** Two cards each. **One face up, one face down.** Antes are posted.
2. **Betting.** Check, call, raise or fold.
3. **Flop** — three community cards. Betting. **Turn** — a fourth. Betting.
   **River** — the fifth. Betting.
4. **Showdown.** Best five-card poker hand from your two plus the five in the
   middle. Standard rankings.

### Why one card is face up

This is the only rule that differs from the poker your friends already know,
and it is there on purpose. Stud has worked this way for a century. It means:

- you can read the table instead of staring at your own cards
- bluffing is something the room can see happening
- the quantum powers have something visible to act on — hiding a card nobody
  could see anyway would be a power with no theatre in it

Your face-up card is labelled **everyone sees**. Your face-down one is
**only you**.

## Candy

Candy is the currency. It is pretend. **There is no money anywhere in this
game**, nothing can be bought, and there is no way to put value in or take it
out. It is a party game that keeps score in sweets.

| Piece | Worth |
|---|---|
| 🍬 Gummy | 1 |
| 🍫 Chocolate | 5 |
| 🍭 Lollipop | 10 |
| 🍯 Candy Apple | 25 |

Your stack shows the fewest pieces that add up, so 47 candy is one apple, two
lollipops and two gummies — and a big stack looks obviously bigger than a small
one from across the room.

Bet buttons are **Nibble** (a third of the pot), **Munch** (two thirds),
**Feast** (the pot), and **ALL CANDY**.

### Trick or Treat

Run out of candy and you are handed more. It is announced, it is counted, and
the amount grows slightly each time you need it.

Nobody sits out a party game watching other people play. The refills appear on
the final scoreboard, so the winner is still the winner.

## The four powers

You get **3 charges** each hand. Powers are entirely optional — ignore all four
and you are playing ordinary Hold'em, and you can win that way.

They arrive one at a time over the first few hands so that nobody meets all of
them at once.

### 👁 Measure — 1 charge · from hand 2
> Reveal a mystery card right now.

Pick any **?** on the table, yours or anyone's, and find out what it is.

### 🔀 Swap — 1 charge · from hand 3
> Trade one of your cards for a new one.

Throw a card back and take whatever comes out. No takebacks.

### 👻 Haunt — 2 charges · from hand 4
> Your card becomes two possibilities at once.

One of your cards turns into a **?** that is secretly either the card you had
or a brand new one. It does not decide until somebody measures it — or until
the showdown, when everything decides at once.

### 🎭 Spectral Bluff — 2 charges · from hand 5
> Your face-up card turns into a ? for everyone else.

You still see it. They do not. There is a **30% chance each street** that it
collapses early and shows them everything, which is the joke.

## ⚡ Quantum Collapse

Now and then the dealer interferes. It is announced with a banner, it happens
to everybody, and there is at most one per hand — never in the first two hands
of the night.

| Event | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Everything Decides At Once** | Every ? on the table resolves |
| **A Card Forgets Itself** | A community card becomes a ? |
| **Entangled!** | Two mystery cards link up — measuring one decides the other |
| **The Board Shuffles Itself** | Two community cards trade places |
| **Candy Rain** | Free candy for everyone still in the hand |

## The monsters

| | Who | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| 🧛 | **The Vampire** | Raises with real hands and raises with nothing. Charming either way. |
| 🧙 | **The Witch** | Casts almost every hand. If she has not cast anything, be worried. |
| 👻 | **The Ghost** | Bets at everything. The hard part is the one time in four when it is real. |
| 🐺 | **The Werewolf** | Bets big, always. Loses a pot and bets bigger. |
| 🐈 | **Schrödinger's Cat** | No tell whatsoever. That is not a flaw, it is the whole bit. |
| 🎃 | **Jack the Pumpkin** | Bets exactly when he has it, and will tell you he has it. |

They are not bluffing at random. Each one estimates how often its hand wins by
actually dealing the rest of the board out a couple of hundred times, then
applies its own personality to the answer. Facing a big bet, Jack folds about
three quarters of the time and the Werewolf about a fifth — a difference you
can notice within a few hands and play against.

## Party mode

Put several names in **Who is playing** and the game passes between them on one
keyboard. A cover screen appears between turns so nobody sees anyone else's
face-down card; press any key to take your turn.

The cover screen is skipped when the previous player has folded or is all in,
because at that point they have nothing left to hide.

## So what is actually quantum about it?

You can stop reading here and lose nothing.

Underneath the costume, a mystery card is a **real qubit** running on the same
state-vector simulator as the main game. A card in superposition is a qubit in
|+⟩: |0⟩ means the first possibility, |1⟩ means the second. Measuring it is a
genuine projective measurement.

Because the qubits are real, **entanglement is real too**. When a Collapse links
two mystery cards, it builds an actual Bell pair — reset both, Hadamard one,
CNOT onto the other. Measuring either one decides both in the same instant,
with no message passing between them, because there is nothing left to send:
the pair never had two separate answers to begin with. The self-checks verify
this over 1,600 deals and it holds every time, in both the "they match" and
"they are opposite" flavours.

The opponents know this too. Their equity sampler rolls each superposition
per trial, weighted by the real amplitudes, and respects the entanglement links
— so a monster facing a **?** is genuinely reasoning about a superposition
rather than ignoring it or quietly peeking.

Each power's tooltip has a **what is this really?** link:

- **Measure** — until you look, the card has no single value. It is not that
  the game is hiding the answer; there is not one yet, and looking is what
  creates it. It is the one move in quantum mechanics you cannot undo.
- **Swap** — a real two-qubit gate, and an expensive one on actual hardware,
  which is why chips are laid out to avoid needing it.
- **Haunt** — superposition. The card is not secretly one of the two while
  pretending to be both. Physicists tested the alternative and ruled it out.
- **Spectral Bluff** — what the other players can know is exactly what the
  state lets them know. Information and physics turn out to be the same
  subject.

None of this is on the critical path to having a good time. That is the point.
